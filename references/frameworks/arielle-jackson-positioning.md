# Arielle Jackson Brand Positioning + Bar Test

## When to Use

Use this when you are an early-stage startup defining your brand for the first time, when 10 employees give 10 different answers about what the company does, or when your marketing copy sounds like it was written by a committee of robots ("leverages," "empowers," "revolutionizes"). Arielle Jackson built this framework across her time at Google, Square, and eight years consulting startups as marketer-in-residence at First Round Capital. The whole process can be completed in about three weeks for a company starting from scratch.

## The Framework

Brand strategy has three components: **Purpose**, **Positioning**, and **Personality**. Together they form a creative brief that drives every downstream decision -- website copy, visual design, tone of voice, hiring, PR.

### Part 1: Purpose

Your purpose is why you do what you do. It makes people want to root for you. It exists on a 10-year frame (positioning can shift every 18 months, but purpose stays).

**Format**: "We exist to ___."

> "A good purpose explains the change you want to see in the world irrespective of financial gain." -- Arielle Jackson

**How to find it**:

1. List all cultural tensions happening in the world relevant to your business (zeitgeist, current events, things your audience subconsciously feels)
2. List all the ways you would describe your brand's best self -- when everything works perfectly, what does it deliver?
3. Pick the best statement from each side
4. With that context, brainstorm: "We exist to ___" and "The world would be a better place if ___"

**Test**: You should be able to say it naturally when introducing yourself at a conference. It should also work as the header for your About page.

> "I want you to go, hi, I'm Lenny, I'm the founder of the company X, and we exist to blah -- and say it and have that feel natural." -- Arielle Jackson

**Examples**:
- **Google**: "To organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful." (Known by every employee from day one of a 100K+ person company.)
- **Stripe**: "To increase the GDP of the internet."
- **Nike**: "To bring inspiration and innovation to every athlete in the world. And if you have a body, you are an athlete."
- **LogicLoop**: "To make operations data work harder than operations people."
- **Woolf**: "To increase access to world-class higher education and ensure that it is globally recognized and transferable."

### Part 2: Positioning

Positioning is the space you occupy in your target customer's mind. You have a positioning problem if you ask 10 customers or employees what the company does and get wildly different answers.

> "You have a positioning problem if I ask 10 of your customers or 10 of your employees what the company does or what the product does and I get multiple answers." -- Arielle Jackson

**The Concentric Circles Model**:

Work from broad to narrow:
1. **TAM** (broadest circle)
2. **Serviceable market**
3. **Target audience** -- who you will outwardly try to acquire for the next 18 months
4. **Model persona** -- an actual person (real or composite) with a name, age, location, job, feelings, priorities

> "With Eero, it was tech savvy dads. And then the model persona was this guy who had teenage kids, lived in suburban St. Louis, had a 2,800 square foot house that was made of brick, worked at home on Fridays. His kids were into gaming. He was the VP of sales at a company that was tech adjacent, but not tech." -- Arielle Jackson

Going narrow is not limiting. It is strategic:

> "When you are an early stage company, the worst thing you can do is try to be everything to everyone because you don't have enough runway." -- Arielle Jackson

**The Positioning Worksheet**:

Answer these questions in order:
1. Who is this for?
2. What is the problem they have?
3. How do they address that problem today?
4. What do you make?
5. How does it work?
6. What would you want a user to tell another person?

That last question is critical. The answer becomes your H1 headline and your word-of-mouth driver.

> "Think about Square Stand and 'turn your iPad into a point of sale.' If I went to a small business owner and they were having drinks with a friend, what would I want them to say? 'Oh yeah, I just got the Square Stand. It's this really cool new register that turns my iPad into a point of sale.' That's pretty much exactly what I want them to say." -- Arielle Jackson

**The Classic 4-Part Positioning Statement**:

All that work distills into a statement that has been in use for 40-50 years:

> "For [target audience] who [statement of need or opportunity], our [product name] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [current alternative], our product [key differentiator]." -- Arielle Jackson

### The Bar Test

The bar test is the validation step that ensures your positioning uses human language, not corporate jargon.

> "One of the really big pitfalls I see, especially for B2B companies, is they write in a way that people don't talk. The document often has things like 'leverages' and 'empowers' and nobody talks like that." -- Arielle Jackson

**How to run it**:

1. Pretend to be someone in your target audience
2. You are having drinks at a bar with another person in your target audience
3. Say out loud: "Hey, I just started using [product name]. It's this really great [category] that [benefit]."
4. The other person says: "Hm, tell me more" or "That's cool, what do you mean?"
5. You respond with your differentiator -- out loud

> "If you run through that test and it's actually stuff people would say out loud, then you've done a pretty good job and you can start using that copy publicly." -- Arielle Jackson

If any phrase would make the other person give you a strange look at a bar, rewrite it. "Blankets your home in fast, reliable wifi" passes. "Leverages proprietary mesh technology to empower connectivity" does not.

### Part 3: Personality

Brand personality determines how you show up in the world -- your written copy, visual design, illustration style, photography choices.

**The Aaker Framework (5 Dimensions)**:

Based on Jennifer Aaker's academic research analyzing top global brands. Every strong brand spikes in two of five dimensions:

1. **Sincerity** -- down to earth, honest
2. **Excitement** -- spirited, daring
3. **Competence** -- reliable, intelligent
4. **Sophistication** -- charming, upper class
5. **Ruggedness** -- outdoorsy, tough

> "Mountain Dew is rugged and exciting. Rolex is sophisticated and competent." -- Arielle Jackson

Most tech companies end up sincerity + competence (Amazon, Google). Apple adds sophistication. Pick your two.

**The 5-Attribute Star**:

After selecting your two dimensions, define five personality attributes with tension. Write them as "We are X, but not Y" where Y is taking X too far.

> "If you tell me we're helpful, we're nice, we're approachable, we're competent, and we're reliable -- you basically haven't told me anything because you just used three words to say one thing and two words to say another thing. Whereas if you tell me, 'We're really savvy, but we're also really approachable' -- that's kind of cool." -- Arielle Jackson

Examples: "We are playful but not silly" (Google). "We are daring but not stupid" (Mountain Dew).

## Example

**Square Stand** (Jackson led the launch as first product marketer at Square):

- **Purpose**: Part of Square's broader mission to make commerce accessible to everyone
- **Positioning**: "For small business owners who need a modern register, Square Stand is a point-of-sale device that turns your iPad into a register. Unlike traditional POS systems that cost thousands and require contracts, Square Stand works out of the box."
- **Word-of-mouth line**: "It turns your iPad into a point of sale."
- **Bar test**: A coffee shop owner tells a restaurant owner friend: "I just got this Square Stand thing -- it turns my iPad into a real point of sale." Natural. Passes.

**Alt** (alternative asset investing platform):

- **Purpose**: "To increase the transparency and liquidity of alternative assets."
- **Positioning**: "Making sports cards as easy to invest in as stocks."
- **Cultural tension**: Millennials nostalgic for the '90s + low interest rates making traditional investments feel stale + rising interest in alternatives

**Eero** (home wifi):

- **Target audience**: Tech savvy dads
- **Model persona**: VP of Sales, suburban St. Louis, 2,800 sq ft brick house, teenage gamers, works from home Fridays
- **Benefit**: "Blankets your home in fast, reliable wifi"

## Output

After applying this framework you walk away with:

- A one-sentence purpose statement ("We exist to ___")
- A concentric circles audience map (TAM down to model persona)
- A positioning statement using the classic 4-part template
- A word-of-mouth line that passes the bar test
- Two brand personality dimensions from the Aaker framework
- Five "X but not Y" personality attributes
- A creative brief you can hand to any designer, writer, or agency

## Timing

> "If you're starting from nothing, I think you can do this in three weeks. The naming process itself can take about a month because of trademark and domains. Whatever time it takes you is actually going to save you so much time down the road. Literally your web copy almost writes itself, if you get this all done right." -- Arielle Jackson
