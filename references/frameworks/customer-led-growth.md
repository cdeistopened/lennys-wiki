# Customer-Led Growth Milestone Mapping

## When to Use

Use this when your free-to-paid conversion is underperforming, your onboarding feels like a dark tunnel between signup and value, or your growth efforts are a collection of disconnected tactics with no shared map. Gia Laudi (co-founder of Forget The Funnel) built this framework after seeing Airbnb's internal customer journey — "Project Snow White," drawn by a full-time Pixar storyboard artist — taped to the wall of their product team's workspace. The framework replaces pirate metrics (AARRR) with a customer-centered journey that marketing, product, and customer success can all align around.

## The Framework

### Step 1: Identify Your Best Customers via Jobs-to-Be-Done Research

Survey or interview customers who meet four criteria: they get real value from the product, they pay, they are low-maintenance, and they signed up recently enough to remember what life was like before.

> "Generally, we say that's in the three to six-month range. Because if you go to somebody that's been your customer for two years, they're just going to fill answers with what they think might have been going on in their life." — Gia Laudi

From the research, identify distinct "customer jobs" — the outcomes people hired your product to achieve. Then prioritize one job to lead with. Prioritization criteria: high willingness to pay, urgent problem (painkiller not vitamin), high retention or expansion potential, and any unfair advantage you have serving that segment.

### Step 2: Map the Customer Journey as Milestones

Build a journey map with roughly six milestones across three phases:

**Struggle Phase** (before they find you):
- **Problem** — They are out in the world experiencing the pain, using the old way. Something has to change.
- **Interest** — They start shopping around, reading reviews, visiting competitor sites.

**Evaluation Phase** (first experience with your product):
- **First Value** — Product activation. They reach the part of the product that convinced them it might solve their problem.
- **Value Realization** — They hit a critical threshold of product engagement. "They reach a point with your product where they're like, 'Hell yes, this is it.'"

**Growth Phase** (continued value):
- **Value Growth** — Frequency and habit building. They reach a healthy usage cadence.
- **Expansion** — They discover what else they need, become advocates, or expand their use.

> "It's the story of how I met and fell in love with your product. It's like this documentary of being out in the world, finding it, realizing that like, 'Hell yeah, this might actually solve a problem for us.'" — Gia Laudi

Keep the number of milestones as low as possible. Gia aims for about six. ("Airbnb had 12. I would hope it's more like six.")

### Step 3: Assign a KPI to Each Milestone

Each milestone needs a measurable indicator so you know whether customers reached it — and so you can catch them when they fall off.

Struggle-phase metrics are straightforward (unique visitors, website conversion rate). Evaluation and growth metrics require tying what customers told you is valuable to specific product attributes.

> "We can associate basically what they told us brings them the most amount of value with the product attribute or parts of the product that deliver that value. We try to tie the KPI obviously to some sort of meaningful product usage of that key part of the product." — Gia Laudi

### Step 4: Build Proactive and Reactive Experiences

For each milestone, design proactive experiences that push customers toward the next value moment (email onboarding, in-app prompts, checklists). Then build reactive "win-back" flows that catch customers who stall.

> "If they haven't even discovered that really, to them, most valued part of their product, you would need to focus on getting them to that value. Otherwise, you can't just keep firing off emails and hoping they're going to jump back into the product." — Gia Laudi

### Step 5: Audit and Realign

With the map and KPIs in place, evaluate every existing marketing and product experience against the milestones. Where is the biggest gap between what you are doing today and what the map says you should be doing? Fix the most broken milestone first.

## Example: SparkToro

SparkToro (Rand Fishkin's audience research tool) had good traffic and signups but weak free-to-paid conversion. Gia's team surveyed recent best-fit customers and identified two customer jobs, then prioritized the marketer job over the data analyst job because SparkToro had an unfair advantage with marketers.

They mapped milestones with specific KPIs:
- **First search performed** — Entry to evaluation
- **5+ searches plus at least one list created** — First value (product activation)
- **Minimum searches + multiple lists + discovering export** — Value realization
- **Sustained engagement at a regular cadence** — Value growth

The research revealed that "lists" and "export" were high-value features that were not front-loaded in the experience. SparkToro's VP of marketing, Amanda Natividad, built a product onboarding checklist and email sequence aligned to the milestones. Result: trial-to-paid conversion doubled within two months, without changing the core product.

Another example: a social media tool where Gia's team updated website messaging based on customer research, shortened the trial from 30 to 7 days, and saw website conversion increase 89%. Trial-to-paid increased 40% without touching anything after signup — simply because better-fit customers were coming through the door.

## Output

A completed milestone map produces:
- A voice-of-customer document with the prioritized customer job
- A messaging and positioning guide (5-7 pages) tied to the job
- A journey map with ~6 milestones across struggle, evaluation, and growth phases
- A KPI for each milestone
- A gap analysis showing which milestones have the weakest experiences today
- A prioritized roadmap of proactive and reactive programs to close the gaps
